LABELLING RUBRIC (concept vocabularies)

Each line of a batch file is one vocabulary item of a language-model tokenizer, met in a Wikipedia article about a
CONCEPT (named in the section header). Format:
    id | token | source words | context
  token          the token as the tokenizer writes it; a leading "▁" marks a word-initial token, no "▁" means the
                 token is a piece from inside a word
  source words   the whole word(s) of the article this token was cut from, most frequent first
  context        one place where it occurs, the token marked [[like this]]

Judge the SOURCE WORD (the whole word the token comes from, first in the list) in the sense it has in the context.
Judge that word itself, not the longer phrase around it (in "a device called a Time Turner", the word "Time" is
generic). If the token is a piece of a longer word (e.g. "wart" from "Hogwarts", "Sean" from "Seanad"), judge the
longer word.

Labels (exactly one per item):
  S  concept-specific. The word names something that belongs to THIS concept in particular: its own named entities
     (people, characters, places, organisations, works, events, products), its technical or in-world terms, or a
     word that mostly occurs when talking about this concept. Someone who forgot the concept would lose this word's
     meaning here.
  A  adjacent. Related to the concept's topic area or to a neighbouring concept, but not specific to this concept;
     it is common in other contexts of the same domain.
  U  unrelated. Generic vocabulary (function words, ordinary verbs/adjectives/nouns, numbers, dates, units),
     names of people, places or institutions that are not part of the concept (e.g. authors of cited studies,
     unrelated cities, companies mentioned in passing), and pieces whose source word you cannot tell.

Worked examples for the concept "Chess" (not part of any batch):
  ▁castling (castling) S      ▁Kasparov (Kasparov) S      ▁FIDE (FIDE) S      ▁checkmate S      ▁pawn S
  ▁tournament A    ▁strategy A    ▁checkers A (neighbouring game)    ▁grandmaster S (chess title)
  ▁Soviet (Soviet, "the Soviet school of chess") A    ▁the U    ▁computed U    ▁Cambridge (a cited publisher) U
  ▁Time (Time, "Time magazine named him") U    "ov" from "Kasparov" -> judge Kasparov -> S

Be decisive; when torn between two labels choose the less specific one (S vs A -> A, A vs U -> U).

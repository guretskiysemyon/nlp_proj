\paragraph{Compute and cost}
All GPU work ran on the university's SLURM cluster, on NVIDIA TITAN Xp and GeForce RTX 2080 Ti nodes, one GPU per job for the jobs of Table~\ref{tab:cost} (one used two); factorizations of the single-language study ran on CPU nodes.
Table~\ref{tab:cost} gives the GPU time allocated to each study and its Gemini spend (Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite).
In the multilingual study, evaluation dominates: \expnum{2.5--3.5} GPU-hours per concept and approach, \expnum{68}, \expnum{34}, \expnum{71} and \expnum{66} GPU-hours for A1, A2, A3 and A3z at fit seed 42, against under a minute per concept for the fits; its seed-42 fits and about \expnum{150} GPU-hours of evaluation (50 units) ran on workstation GPUs that the accounting does not record.
The single-language feature-level ladder spent USD \expnum{2.03} on feature judging (TJ, WJ, lexicons and a 10\% re-judge for noise) and USD \expnum{2.41} on Alpaca coherence for its erasure anchor; the collateral-aware evaluation used Gemini only for Alpaca coherence (USD \expnum{1.92} in development, \expnum{2.24} for the seed-42 last-mile check and \expnum{1.54} at the integrity-gated points), since its questions and all free-text labels were written by LLM agents (Claude) instead; the multilingual study spent USD \expnum{2.24} on selection (judge and lexicon calls), \expnum{2.16} on generating and verifying the questions of the 20 new concepts and \expnum{0.20} on the stability study.
The spend is logged with deliberately conservative prices (USD \expnum{0.30} or \expnum{0.25} per million input and \expnum{2.50} per million output tokens), so it is an upper bound.

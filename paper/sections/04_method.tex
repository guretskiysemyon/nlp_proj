%=====================================================================
\section{\cinder{}}
\label{sec:method}
%=====================================================================

\cinder{} keeps \ember{}'s data, Sparse MF, $\rho$ filter and edit form (Eq.~\ref{eq:edit}), and changes how features are decomposed (\S\ref{sec:cs}), identified (\S\ref{sec:twj}) and erased (\S\ref{sec:cinderz}); \S\ref{sec:ml} extends it to many languages with one fit.

\subsection{Finding Concept Features in a Sharpened Space}
\label{sec:cs}

Sparse MF finds the directions with the most energy; rather than changing the factorization, we change the matrix so that the concept carries that energy, combining whitening, a standard step before sparse coding \citep{hyvarinen2000ica}, with a target--background contrast \citep{abid2018exploring}.

\paragraph{Contrastive Sharpening}
We \emph{whiten} the embeddings against ordinary text, with the mean $\mu_B$ and covariance $\Sigma_B$ of the token types of a large Wikipedia sample (computed once per model), and \emph{sharpen} them toward the whitened token types $\tilde{\mathbf u}_1,\dots,\tilde{\mathbf u}_m$ of the concept's Wikipedia page:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t &= W(\e_t - \mu_B), \quad W = (\Sigma_B + \varepsilon I)^{-1/2},\\
  \mathbf{x}_t &= S_\eta^{\gamma/2}\,\tilde{\mathbf u}_t, \quad
  S_\eta = (1-\eta)\,\tfrac{1}{m}\textstyle\sum_{j} \tilde{\mathbf u}_j \tilde{\mathbf u}_j^{\top} + \eta I ,
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:whiten}
\end{equation}
with $\eta = 0.05$ and $\gamma = 1$; Sparse MF then factorizes $X = T(A - \mu_B\mathbf 1^{\top}) \approx \tilde Z Y$ with $T = S_\eta^{\gamma/2} W$.
After whitening, the page's second moment along a direction measures how much more the concept uses it than ordinary text does, and sharpening multiplies the energy along each such direction by this ratio raised to $\gamma$, as in Fisher's discriminant and contrastive PCA \citep{fisher1936use,abid2018exploring} (Proposition~\ref{prop:cs}, \S\ref{app:cs}).
Whitening removes the component that rare tokens share, so the concept's tokens separate from other rare tokens, but makes every direction equally strong; sharpening then makes the clean cluster worth a feature (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).
On Llama, a single page yields few directions above sampling noise; pooling the pages of eight languages yields many more (\S\ref{app:cs-llama}).
Since $T$ is invertible, the factorization maps back exactly: $\cvec_t = T^{-1}\tilde\cvec_t$.

\paragraph{Global Sparsity}
\ember{}'s row sparsity (RS) bounds every feature separately, $\|\mathbf y_i\|_0 \le s\,n$; Global Sparsity (\gs{}) bounds the whole matrix with the same total budget, $\|Y\|_0 \le s\,k\,n$.
After each dense update of $Y$, \gs{} keeps the $s\,k\,n$ entries with the largest share of their row, $|y_{ij}| / \|\mathbf y_i\|_2$, with a floor of $0.2\%$ and a cap of $3\%$ of the tokens per feature.
For this score, it is the exact projection onto the constraint, as in iterative hard thresholding \citep{blumensath2009iht}, and mirrors across features what BatchTopK does across examples \citep{bussmann2024batchtopk}; the scale-free score keeps a few high-energy generic features from taking the budget (\S\ref{app:gs}).

\subsection{Identifying Concept Features from Both Directions}
\label{sec:twj}

\ember{}'s token judge (TJ) works bottom-up, abstracting a feature into a sentence, and fails when the abstraction drops the concept.
Inspired by query expansion \citep{wang2023query2doc,gao2023hyde}, the Two-Way Judge (\twj{}) adds a top-down path: once per concept, before any feature is judged, an LLM expands the concept into a lexicon $L_C$ of 20 words specific to it rather than to its broader category.
Each feature's top tokens are mapped to the words they come from in $\Sc$ (e.g., \textit{ffindor} $\to$ \textit{Gryffindor}), giving a word list $W_i$, and the expansion judge accepts the feature if $|W_i \cap L_C| \ge t$ (EJ@$t$); \twj{} accepts if either path does ($\text{TJ} \vee \text{EJ@}5$).
The union raises recall, $t$ controls precision, and the lexicon path adds no LLM call per feature (\S\ref{app:twj}).

\subsection{Erasing With and Without the Mean}
\label{sec:cinderz}

For fixed coefficients, the least-squares dictionary does not depend on the metric in which the error is measured (Proposition~\ref{prop:metric}, \S\ref{app:edit}), so whitening does not distort the mapped-back edit; what differs from \ember{} is the centring.
\cinder{}'s concept part describes how a token departs from ordinary text, whereas \ember{}'s features also absorb a share of $\mu_B$, the component that all tokens share (\expnum{44\%} of its edit's energy on Gemma), which the model's first layer reads strongly (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).
\cinderz{} keeps the sharpened fit's coefficients $Y$, selected features and edited tokens, and re-solves only the dictionary on the raw embeddings,
\begin{equation}
  Z_{z} = A\,Y^{\top}\big(Y Y^{\top} + \lambda I\big)^{-1},
  \label{eq:cinderz}
\end{equation}
a $k \times k$ solve with no new fit or judge call that adds exactly a mean term, $\cvec^{z}_t = \cvec_t + \beta_t\,\mu_B$ (Proposition~\ref{cor:mean}).

\subsection{One Fit for Eight Languages}
\label{sec:ml}

\cinder{} erases a concept in eight languages with one fit on 30 concept and 30 neutral sentences per language, the fewest that yield a full-rank matrix.
Here the concept also competes with language identity, which multilingual models encode in a few dominant directions \citep{chang2022geometry,libovicky2020language}, so \cinder{} whitens against a background with equal text in every language, which weakens the language directions about threefold relative to an English background, and sharpens toward the concept's pages pooled over all languages.
With the dictionary fixed, it solves the coefficients of every token in the full multilingual sentence sets, selects features with \twj{}, whose lexicon covers all languages in one LLM call, and builds the edit as in \cinderz{}.
The saving is in selection: per-language \ember{} runs eight factorizations and eight judging passes per concept (\expnum{160} judge calls), \cinder{} one of each (\expnum{20} calls), about a fifth of the factorization work and a third of the LLM cost; GPU fit time and evaluation, which dominates, cost about the same (\S\ref{app:ml}).

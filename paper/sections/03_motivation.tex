%=====================================================================
\section{Why \ember{} Misses Quiet Concepts}
\label{sec:motivation}
%=====================================================================

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/mechanism.pdf}
  \caption{Why \ember{} misses quiet concepts (Gemma-2-2B-it). (a)~Rarer tokens share a larger component along the background mean $\hat\mu_B$ (median and interquartile range per frequency bin). (b)~A sparse feature seeded on each concept's lexicon tokens and run to its fixed point, per concept (small markers) with medians (large): in the raw matrix it is valuable but drifts to other rare tokens; after whitening it is pure but ordinary; after sharpening, pure and valuable.}
  \label{fig:mechanism}
\end{figure*}

How reliably does \ember{} find the target concept?
We reproduced it on Gemma-2-2B-it, matching the published accuracies within four points (\S\ref{app:ember}), inspected each stage on its flagship concept, Harry Potter, and tested most observations on 38 concepts and five seeds.
We find five issues, which Figure~\ref{fig:overview} maps to \cinder{}'s changes.

\paragraph{The concept hides behind rarity}
Sparse MF minimizes squared reconstruction error, so a feature is worth the energy it explains.
Concept tokens carry as much energy as neutral ones but are rarer (in \expnum{35 of 38} concepts), and in Gemma's embeddings rare tokens share a large component along the background mean $\mu_B$ (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}a; cf.\ \citealp{gao2019representation}).
\ember{}'s concept feature is therefore loud for the wrong reason: \expnum{42\%} of its squared norm lies along $\hat\mu_B$, against \expnum{12\%} for a typical feature, and it groups the concept with unrelated rare tokens, which \ember{} then edits (\S\ref{app:examples}).
Whitening removes the shared component but leaves a pure, ordinary cluster, which the factorization finds less often than before (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}b): in this sense the concept is \emph{quiet}.

\paragraph{One width does not fit all concepts}
\ember{} keeps the top 1\% of tokens, about 48, in every feature, but the \emph{natural tail}, where tokens ranked by their share of the concept feature stop being specific to the concept's page, lies beyond this cut for \expnum{15} of 38 concepts and before it for another \expnum{15} (\S\ref{app:gs}).

\paragraph{The judge loses the concept in its summary}
The judge decides from a one-sentence description, and the description is where concepts get lost: Heroin and World War II features became ``substances, medications, and illicit drugs [\dots]'' and ``20th-century totalitarian political ideologies [\dots]'', and the judge ruled them not to be the concept (\S\ref{app:examples}).
A rejected concept is not erased at all; on 12 held-out concepts, \ember{} edits nothing in \expnum{19 of 60} runs.

\paragraph{The edit stays in English}
On Gemma, \ember{}-edited models retain \expnum{0.19--0.24} more of the concept's knowledge in German, Spanish, French and Russian than in English (\S\ref{app:ml}).
A single \ember{} fit on multilingual text accepts a concept feature for only \expnum{10 of 38} concepts on Llama-3.1-8B, mostly at the judge; the means of the eight languages carry \expnum{34} times the energy of the concept, which is itself about \expnum{3} times quieter than in English.

\paragraph{The evaluation rewards destroying tokens}
$H$ selects the largest strength, $\delta = 200$, for \expnum{6 of the 10} concepts \ember{} edits in our reproduction.
The edited embedding is then 40--120 times longer than the original and points along $-\cvec_t$: the word is replaced rather than cleaned.
No metric registers this: the concept questions contain the edited words, so a destroyed word counts as erased knowledge, the similar-domain questions avoid them, the MMLU gate never binds in any of the \expnum{344} grid points we scored, and Alpaca coherence stays at or above \expnum{0.92}.
Free text does: on Harry Potter at $\delta = 200$, \expnum{12 of 20} prompts, most using the edited words in other senses, break, against \expnum{0--1} at $\delta = 1$ (Table~\ref{tab:examples}): the regime of \emph{token destruction}.
